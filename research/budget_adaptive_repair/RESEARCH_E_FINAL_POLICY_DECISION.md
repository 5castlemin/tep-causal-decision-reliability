# Research E — Final Policy Decision

No universally optimal repair budget or policy is established. The defensible operational interpretation is conditional: use task-relevant repair as a candidate prioritization principle when reference structure and task are available, and consider additional edits only while expected decision benefit justifies their edit burden under the stated objective. The experiments do not provide a validated rule for estimating that benefit in a real manufacturing line.

Frozen M4 remains the conservative reference-assisted baseline. T5-guided M4 remains a candidate with an encouraging but imprecise S4 point estimate; its independent replication interval includes zero and misses its precision target, while S5 had no observed first-edit disagreement. No superiority or noninferiority claim for T5 is warranted. Full Greedy can achieve stronger structural repair at greater edit counts, but its decision value varies by cohort and objective. Graph-free association is competitive in S5 when cost is counted only as graph edits.

The lambda lower envelope is a descriptive sensitivity analysis on observed means. It supports illustrating how assumed edit aversion changes the selected policy-budget point; it does not identify the actual cost preference of a firm. Research E's original C-series B=0–9 results remain the deeper saturation analysis. The extension did not perform B=5–9 budget replay for S4 or S5 because their preserved sealed results cover only B=0–4. Their apparent B=4 plateaus must not be generalized.

This is a decision-support research result, not a deployment or safety recommendation. Reference DAG access, synthetic/semisynthetic data, fixed training frames, task targets and edge-edit-only cost are central assumptions.
