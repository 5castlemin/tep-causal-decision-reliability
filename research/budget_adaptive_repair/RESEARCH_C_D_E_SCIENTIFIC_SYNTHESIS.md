# Research C–D–E scientific synthesis

## Evidence layers

**Research C (frozen core):** On 200 independent synthetic SCMs in Held-out, the B2 Primary `Regret(S0)−Regret(Full Greedy)` was `+0.07483636` (paired-t 95% CI `[0.05089822, 0.09877450]`). This supports the original frozen contract's average comparison within its designed synthetic population. M4 remains a Development-selected Secondary.

**Mechanism analysis (post hoc):** Original-ledger audits support that absent task paths and task-zone repair often co-occurred with decision changes, but examples also show effect-sign repair without path restoration, off-task errors affecting task paths or DAG feasibility, and repaired structure worsening action. It remains exploratory even when checked against original Ledgers.

**Research D (conditional external semisynthetic):** On one DRF-based DGP and fixed training frame, B2 `L(S0)−L(Full Greedy)=+0.03422490` across 200 graph-corruption draws (two-source 95% interval `[0.00617347,0.06535080]`). Both MC precision targets were missed. Graph-free association attained the same mean loss as Full Greedy; M4 used fewer edge edits but had slightly higher loss on two draws. This is not physical manufacturing validation.

**Research E (post hoc budget/cost extension):** Existing C inputs show early diminishing returns for task-centric repair, non-monotone behavior for some policies, and finite budget-dependent Pareto trade-offs. Added budgets reuse the same C SCMs only. Adaptive M4 and target sensitivity are exploratory C-only extensions. D E1 is aggregate-only; D extensions needing unit records were unavailable.

## Joint interpretation

The most defensible conclusion is conditional: in the frozen synthetic experiments, task-relevant structural repair often achieved large decision improvements with fewer edge edits than broader repair, but extra budget can have diminishing or negative returns, graph-free association competed strongly in D, and neither structural accuracy nor task-centric repair guarantees better decisions on every unit. Research E narrows the conditions under which budget allocation appears efficient; it does not establish a universal policy, a real-world cost function, or a manufacturing deployment effect.

Research C's SCM-level inference, Research D's fixed-DGP graph-corruption inference and Research E's post-hoc budget analyses have different sampling units and estimands. Their absolute loss values must not be pooled.
